What Drives the Price of an Airbnb Night?

A data investigation across 5 major global cities — London, Rome, Bangkok, Barcelona, and Amsterdam — using over 139,000 listings scraped in March 2026.

Dataset Overview

About the Data

The dataset was compiled from Inside Airbnb and obtained via Kaggle. It covers Airbnb listings scraped in March 2026 across major world cities. After filtering for valid price data, our working dataset contains 139,950 listings across 5 cities.

All prices are expressed in USD equivalent. Note: Bangkok prices required a currency correction from Thai Baht to USD — a key data quality finding of this project.

Insight 01

Room Type and City are the Strongest Price Drivers

Across all 5 cities, the price ordering by room type is consistent and universal:

Hotel room — Highest Entire home/apt Private room Shared room — Lowest

This pattern holds regardless of city, confirming that what type of space you rent matters more than where it is located. Amsterdam charges the highest median prices for entire homes (250 USD) and London leads for hotel rooms (280 USD).

London hotel rooms command nearly 280 USD per night median — while Bangkok hotel rooms cost just 47 USD, highlighting the dual influence of both room type and city.

Fig 1. Median nightly price by room type and city. Median used instead of mean to avoid distortion from extreme outliers.

Insight 02

Bangkok is a Global Budget Outlier — and a Data Quality Lesson

Bangkok stands clearly apart from all European cities in terms of nightly price. After discovering and correcting a currency error (Bangkok prices were stored in Thai Baht rather than USD), Bangkok's median price of approximately 35 USD per night is roughly 4-6x cheaper than any European city in the dataset.

Data Science Lesson: Always validate your data before drawing conclusions. The uncorrected data showed Bangkok as the most expensive city — completely wrong. Careful investigation revealed the currency issue and allowed us to correct it.

This insight highlights an important principle — data quality investigation is as important as the analysis itself.

Fig 2. KDE distribution of nightly prices by city on a logarithmic scale. Bangkok (green) clearly clusters at much lower prices than European cities.

Insight 03

Professional Hosts Charge More Than Casual Hosts

A counterintuitive finding: professional hosts (managing 6+ listings) consistently charge higher median prices than casual hosts (1-5 listings) in almost every city.

59.3% Casual Hosts 40.7% Professional Hosts

One might expect professional operators to offer more competitive prices due to economies of scale. Instead, the opposite is true — professional hosts price higher, possibly because they offer higher quality properties, have optimized their pricing strategy, and tend to list entire homes rather than spare rooms.

The gap is most pronounced in London (~50 USD difference) and Barcelona (~45 USD difference), while Amsterdam and Bangkok show minimal differences.

Fig 3. Median nightly price comparison between professional and casual hosts by city.

Insight 04

Amsterdam is Surprisingly Expensive Despite Being the Smallest Market

Amsterdam dominates the top 10 most expensive neighbourhoods globally, claiming 8 out of 10 spots. This is remarkable given that Amsterdam has the fewest listings (5,874) of all 5 cities in our dataset.

De Pijp - Rivierenbuurt is the single most expensive neighbourhood in our entire dataset at approximately 250 USD per night median — beating all London and European neighbourhoods.

Amsterdam's Airbnb market is characterized by a concentration of listings in premium central neighbourhoods, high demand relative to limited supply due to strict housing regulations, and a market where scarcity drives prices up despite lower overall listing volume.

Fig 4. Top 10 most expensive neighbourhoods globally (minimum 50 listings). Amsterdam dominates with 8 out of 10 spots.

Insight 05

The 4-7 Night Sweet Spot Commands Premium Prices

Listings requiring 4-7 night minimum stays tend to command the highest nightly prices across most European cities. After this sweet spot, prices generally decline for longer minimum stay requirements.

1 night — Standard 2-3 nights — Slight rise 4-7 nights — Peak prices 8-30 nights — Declining 30+ nights — Lowest

The 4-7 night bracket corresponds to full week rentals that attract higher-value tourists. Listings requiring 30+ nights are effectively long-term rentals where hosts discount their nightly rate to secure guaranteed longer bookings.

This insight is useful for hosts: requiring a 4-7 night minimum may allow charging higher nightly rates while still attracting quality guests.

Fig 5. Median nightly price trend across minimum stay brackets by city. Points show median price, lines show the trend direction.

Insight 06

Popular Listings Converge Toward Mid-Range Pricing

Listings with more reviews tend to have less price variance and converge toward the 100-300 USD range in European cities. This suggests a natural market self-correction mechanism.

Listings with very few reviews (1-3) show the highest price variance — new listings have not yet found their optimal market price. As listings accumulate more reviews, extreme prices become less common — the market naturally filters out unreasonably priced listings over time.

This suggests that experience and market feedback play an important role in price optimization — hosts learn from their booking history and adjust prices accordingly over time.

Fig 6. Price vs. number of reviews scatter plot by city (log-log scale, 5,000 random listings sampled for clarity).

Insight 07

Booking Activity Affects Price Differently in Each City

The relationship between recent booking activity and price varies dramatically by city - with no universal pattern across markets.

Barcelona - more activity = higher price Amsterdam - more activity = lower price London - gentle decline Rome - stable throughout Bangkok - no effect

In Barcelona, the most actively booked listings charge the highest prices - statistically confirmed by narrow confidence intervals throughout. In Amsterdam, the opposite is true - popular listings are actually cheaper, though this finding carries more uncertainty.

Practical tip for guests: In Barcelona, popular listings cost more - book early. In Amsterdam, popular listings may actually offer better value than less reviewed ones.

This finding was confirmed using a lineplot with 95% confidence intervals, showing that booking activity is a city-specific price signal rather than a universal one.

Fig 7. Median nightly price trend by recent booking activity level per city. Shaded areas represent 95% confidence intervals. Created using sns.lineplot.

Conclusion

What Really Drives Airbnb Prices?

Our investigation across 139,950 listings in 5 global cities reveals that Airbnb nightly prices are driven by a combination of factors — none acting alone. Through 7 key insights we uncovered both universal patterns and surprising city-specific dynamics.

Room type is the most universal driver, consistently separating prices across all cities. City and neighbourhood matter enormously at the global level, with Bangkok offering prices 4-6x lower than European cities, and Amsterdam's central neighbourhoods commanding the highest prices of all.

Host type, minimum stay requirements, and review activity all play secondary but meaningful roles — professional hosts charge more, the 4-7 night sweet spot commands premium prices, and popular listings naturally converge toward market-fair pricing over time.
